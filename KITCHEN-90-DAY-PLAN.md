# Ruchitva Kitchen — the thirty subscriber plan

A ninety-day operating plan for ₹2,00,000 of borrowed money and one full-time
founder. Costed against the real prices in `app/shared/plans.ts`: ₹2,800
breakfast, ₹4,800 breakfast + lunch, 22 weekday deliveries inside 5 km of
Kompally.

**The only number that matters: 24 subscribers.** That is break-even with you
cooking, two helpers, a part-time rider and the loan EMI. Everything in the next
ninety days either moves toward it or is a distraction.

## 1. What the ₹2,00,000 buys

| Line | Amount | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen equipment | ₹55,000 | Stove, wet grinder, steamer, fridge (second-hand fine), prep tables, vessels, **digital scale** |
| Steel carriers + insulated bags | ₹22,000 | Two sets per subscriber for ~40 subs. Steel ≈ ₹5/meal vs ₹12 disposable; pays back in ten weeks at 30 subs |
| Raw material float — one month | ₹27,000 | Buy millets monthly in bulk (Bowenpally / Begum Bazaar), never in 2 kg kirana packets |
| Trials & local marketing | ₹25,000 | Sampling in three gated communities, gate standee, menu cards with WhatsApp QR |
| Delivery | ₹18,000 | Second-hand scooter / deposit + fuel float. You deliver for the first sixty days |
| Licences & compliance | ₹8,000 | FSSAI Basic Registration, trade licence. File in week one |
| App, domain, printing, UPI signage | ₹5,000 | The subscription app is already built — keep this line small |
| **Cash reserve — do not touch** | **₹40,000** | Six months EMI plus one shock. Kitchens fail from running out of cash in month four, not from bad food |

**The EMI is a fixed cost from day one.** ₹2 lakh at ~14% over 3 years ≈
**₹6,850/month** regardless of sales — about four subscribers' worth of
break-even. Substitute your actual sanctioned rate and tenure.

## 2. What one breakfast actually costs

₹2,800 ÷ 22 = **₹127 revenue per breakfast.**

| Line | Disposable | Steel |
|---|---:|---:|
| Millet / grain ~110 g | ₹10 | ₹10 |
| Dal, vegetables, chutney or sambar | ₹12 | ₹12 |
| Oil, spices, LPG | ₹6 | ₹6 |
| Packaging | ₹12 | ₹5 |
| **Food + packaging** | **₹40** | **₹33** |

Food cost 31% of revenue (26% on steel) — healthy; under 35% works.
**Contribution per breakfast subscriber = ₹2,800 − (22 × ₹40) = ₹1,920/month.**
That is what one new customer is worth.

### The lunch plan is underpriced — fix before selling it

The combo adds ₹2,000 for 22 lunches = **₹91/meal**. Millet rice + dal + curry +
buttermilk + a bigger container costs ~₹65. ₹26 of margin does not survive a
second afternoon delivery run.

Pick one:
1. **Deliver lunch in the same morning run** in an insulated carrier — the
   standard Hyderabad tiffin-carrier model, one rider, no new cost. *Recommended.*
2. **Reprice the combo to ₹5,400** — lunch at ₹118/meal, ₹53 margin.

Update `shared/plans.ts` once decided.

## 3. Break-even moves every time you hire

| Staffing | Fixed cost / month | Break-even |
|---|---:|---:|
| You cook, one helper, you deliver | ₹26,850 | **14 subs** |
| You cook, two helpers, part-time rider | ₹45,850 | **24 subs** |
| Hired cook, two helpers, two riders, rented kitchen | ₹1,01,850 | **54 subs** |

**The decision that doubles your income:** at 60 subscribers (40 breakfast, 20
combo), a hired cook + rented kitchen clears ~₹25,000/month. You still cooking,
three helpers, one rider, home kitchen clears ~₹58,700/month on identical
revenue. Stay at the stove as long as the law and the volume allow. Your cost
advantage *is* your labour, and it just went full-time.

## 4. The ninety days

### Days 1–10 — Licence, cost, one building
- File **FSSAI Basic Registration** day one. Budget for the State Licence at
  ~40 subs, when turnover crosses ₹12 lakh.
- **Cook one batch of each of the five dishes and weigh everything.** Grams in,
  portions out, cost per plate. Every pricing decision for two years rests here.
- **Pick one gated community inside 3 km.** Get association permission to sample
  at the gate. One building, not five — eight subs in one tower costs ₹5/drop,
  eight scattered across Kompally costs ₹40.
- Separate current account. Household money and kitchen money never touch again.

**Done when:** FSSAI filed, costing sheet written, one building agreed.

### Days 11–25 — Twenty households, five free breakfasts
- 20 households × 5 free days = 100 meals ≈ ₹4,000. Cheapest acquisition you
  will ever buy.
- **Take the phone number on day one of the trial, not day five.** Enter them
  into the app as trials from the first meal.
- Deliver all hundred personally. Ask at the door: *"ఎలా ఉంది?"* and "what would
  you change?" Write answers down the same morning.
- **Close on day five, in person.** ₹1,499 for the first fortnight (11 days),
  then ₹2,800/month. A fortnight is an easier yes.
- Expect 6–9 of 20 to convert. That is a good trial.

**Done when:** 8 paying subscribers.

### Days 26–55 — To twenty-four (break-even)
- **Build the 20-day menu now** (see rule 1). Most urgent product task you have.
- **Referral, spoken at the door:** 4 free delivery days for any subscriber who
  brings a neighbour onto a paid plan. Costs ₹160 of food, earns ₹1,920 of
  contribution. Strongest channel you have inside a gated community.
- **Open the second building only when the first is at 12 subscribers.**
- Second helper at 18 subs. Part-time rider at 25 — not before.
- Start the **WhatsApp broadcast**: tomorrow's menu, 7 PM daily. Two minutes,
  highest-value marketing habit in this business.

**Done when:** 24 subscribers — the kitchen pays for itself.

### Days 56–90 — Hold what you have, then add lunch
- **Measure renewal rate and treat it as the only score.** Of everyone whose
  first month ended, what % paid for a second? Below 70% = product problem, and
  adding customers just pours water into a leaking pot.
- **Call every non-renewal.** A call, not a WhatsApp message. Three calls teach
  more than three months of guessing.
- **Launch lunch** (repriced or morning-carrier). Sell to existing breakfast
  subscribers first — the sale costs nothing.
- **Swiggy / Zomato for idle capacity only** — à la carte ₹149–₹199, never the
  subscription.
- Target 35–40 subscribers by day 90 across two or three buildings.

**Done when:** 35+ subscribers, renewal rate above 70%.

## 5. Five rules that decide whether this works

1. **The menu repeats every five days — fix it before day 45.** `menuFallback.ts`
   has five days: రాగి దోశ every Monday, forever. By week three she has eaten the
   same dosa four times; by week five she quits and cannot say why. Build a
   **20-day rotation** — four distinct weeks. You do not need twenty recipes;
   vary the chutney, the accompaniment and the millet. Menu monotony is the
   number one killer of tiffin subscriptions and it kills quietly in month two.

2. **Sell buildings, not a city.** Thirty subs across Kompally is a losing
   business; thirty in three towers is a good one. Delivery falls from ~₹40 to
   under ₹8 a meal, one rider covers the run in ninety minutes, and word of mouth
   compounds in the lift. Politely refuse orders outside the cluster for six
   months — that is a growth decision, not a lost sale.

3. **Aggregators are for leftover capacity, never subscriptions.** 18–30% of a
   ₹127 meal is your whole margin, and they train customers to be one-off buyers
   comparing you to forty kitchens on price. A subscriber is worth ₹1,920/month
   and renews; an aggregator customer is worth ₹35 once.

4. **Money in advance is not money earned.** ₹2,800 on the 1st is thirty days of
   food you still owe. Never let the balance drop below one month of ingredients
   plus one EMI. Write three numbers every evening — meals delivered, money in,
   money out. Ten minutes a day.

5. **Right to drop AI video, wrong to drop video.** Generic AI ads are worthless
   *because* everyone can make them. But AI cannot generate your actual dosa,
   your actual steam, your actual hands — real footage just became the scarce
   thing. Shoot 30 seconds on your phone every morning: batter hitting the tawa,
   the carrier packed at 6:40 AM, the ragi weighed. Zero cost, uncopyable, and
   unmistakably not synthetic. In Telugu, to Kompally — not to the internet.

## 6. Months four to twelve

| Move | Why it works | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| More towers in the same 5 km | Same kitchen, same run, same rider; a repeat of a process proved twice. ₹1,920 per sub mostly drops through | First |
| Office / corporate breakfast | One drop point, 20–40 meals, one invoice, no per-door cost. Highest-margin order type. Needs the FSSAI State Licence | Second |
| Millet snack boxes & festival orders | Idle afternoon hours. సంక్రాంతి, ఉగాది and wedding orders are pure upside on fixed costs already paid, sold to the existing WhatsApp list at zero acquisition cost | Third |

Talk to a CA before crossing **₹20 lakh annual turnover** (~60 subs on combo) —
GST registration is much cheaper to plan for than to be caught by.

## 7. The YouTube question — AI news vs AI movies

**AI news channel:** near-zero production cost, thin competition in Telugu, no
synthetic-content policy risk, skill compounds — but a daily treadmill with no
back catalogue; a video is worthless in 72 hours.

**AI movies channel:** evergreen, but Veo/Sora credits cost money you borrowed,
hours of generation per usable minute, and it sits directly in the path of
YouTube's inauthentic-content rules. Everyone with a prompt box is doing it —
the same reason you dropped AI ads.

**If forced to pick: AI news, in Telugu.** But run the arithmetic first. A Telugu
channel at 100,000 views/month earns roughly **₹4,000–₹8,000** — three breakfast
subscribers, which is one morning of sampling in a building you already deliver
to. Reaching that takes 12–18 months of near-daily work and earns **₹0** for the
first six (not monetisable until 1,000 subs and 4,000 watch hours).

Your scarce resource is not the ₹2 lakh. It is your attention, now committed
full-time to a kitchen that needs 24 customers to survive. A second content
business in the same ninety days does not double your income — it halves the
chance the kitchen reaches break-even.

**The channel that earns its place: Ruchitva Kitchen, in Telugu.** Millet
recipes, what a cloud kitchen morning looks like at 5 AM, "చిరుధాన్యాలు ఎలా
వండాలి", honest numbers on starting a tiffin service with ₹2 lakh. Every video
sells subscriptions *and* builds toward monetisation, where an AI news channel
does one job and competes with the kitchen for your mornings. You already have
the accounts, and you have what no prompt can generate: a real kitchen, running.

Revisit passive income at 60 subscribers. Not before.
